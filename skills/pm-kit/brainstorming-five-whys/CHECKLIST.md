# Five Whys — Acceptance Checklist

- [ ] The problem statement is concrete, observable, and bounded in time (not a general complaint or a category of problems).
- [ ] Each "why?" answer introduces a new causal layer — it does not restate or synonymize the previous answer.
- [ ] The causal chain reaches a root cause rather than stopping at a symptom or intermediate cause.
- [ ] The root cause is actionable: a team or process can be changed to address it.
- [ ] The root cause does not attribute blame to a named individual or group of people.
- [ ] At least two corrective actions are proposed, each targeting the root cause rather than a downstream symptom.
- [ ] Each corrective action is assigned to a role or team and has a target date or sprint.
- [ ] The output cites the canonical Five Whys reference URL (`https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/5-whys/`).
- [ ] The "Source consulted" section records which mode was used (offline or online) and does not include fabricated quotations or page numbers.
- [ ] The facilitation dialogue is in the language at `language.communication`, and the saved artifact is in the language at `language.output` (falling back to `language.communication` if `language.output` is absent or empty).
- [ ] The artifact is saved to `docs/pm-kit/outputs/brainstorming-five-whys/<slug>.md` and begins with the three-line provenance header.

> Mark **PASS** or **FAIL**. On **FAIL**, return to the skill with notes.
