# Resource Constraints Ideation — Acceptance Checklist

- [ ] The binding constraint is stated as a single, concrete, measurable sentence — not a vague concern or a general category of difficulty.
- [ ] At least three "options within the constraint" are identified, each specifying what would be kept and what would be eliminated.
- [ ] At least one analogy-inspired option is drawn from a domain outside the current project context.
- [ ] The extreme-constraint thought experiment produced a clearly identified highest-leverage option.
- [ ] Each option in the scoring table has a feasibility rating and an impact rating, and the top options are ranked.
- [ ] The action plan includes at least two actions, each assigned to a role or team with a target date or sprint.
- [ ] Each action operates within the stated constraint — no action assumes the constraint will be lifted.
- [ ] The output cites the canonical reference URL (`https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_constraints`).
- [ ] The "Source consulted" section records which mode was used (offline or online) and does not include fabricated quotations or page numbers.
- [ ] The facilitation dialogue is in the language at `language.communication`, and the saved artifact is in the language at `language.output` (falling back to `language.communication` if `language.output` is absent or empty).
- [ ] The artifact is saved to `docs/pm-kit/outputs/brainstorming-resource-constraints/<slug>.md` and begins with the three-line provenance header.

> Mark **PASS** or **FAIL**. On **FAIL**, return to the skill with notes.
